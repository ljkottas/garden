About GARDEN

Intentional evangelism for ordinary Christians.

GARDEN exists to help believers become more intentional with the people God has already placed in their lives—without requiring them to become professional apologists, gifted debaters, or fearless conversationalists first.

How This Started

GARDEN grew out of a personal conviction.

I was preparing for something I love: a backcountry hunting trip. Preparing well for a hunt requires far more than simply showing up in the mountains. I can spend an extraordinary amount of time studying a mountain range—looking at terrain, basins, drainages, water sources, access routes, weather, and the areas most likely to hold animals.

Then there is everything I will carry. The pack. Gear. Clothing. Food. Water. Weight. What I need, what I can leave behind, and what could go wrong. Long before the hunt begins, I may have spent countless hours thinking through details most people would never know existed.

While doing that preparation, I was struck by a simple question:

If I am willing to put this much thought and effort into a hobby I care about, what would it look like to bring that kind of intentionality to the people I care about who do not know Christ?

That question stayed with me.

The first response was very simple. I began adding friends and family members to my prayer journal. I kept a few notes about conversations we had, things they believed, questions they raised, and little seeds of truth I had been able to share—what Greg Koukl has memorably described as putting a “stone in the shoe.”

There was no website. No framework. No toolkit. It was simply an attempt to become more intentional.

Over time, that conviction and habit grew into what became GARDEN.

The Mission

The mission of GARDEN is to help ordinary Christians engage the people already in their lives with greater intentionality, confidence, wisdom, and faithfulness over time.

Many Christians care deeply about evangelism but feel unprepared for spiritual conversations. They may be afraid of being asked a question they cannot answer, uncomfortable discussing controversial subjects, or convinced that effective evangelism requires a level of biblical, philosophical, or apologetic knowledge they do not possess.

GARDEN begins somewhere different.

You do not need to know the answer in the moment. You can listen. You can think later. You can learn when the pressure is gone. You can return to the conversation another day.

That simple shift removes much of the pressure that keeps believers silent.

The Long Game

GARDEN is built around a relational view of evangelism. Most people are not going to reconsider their worldview because of one perfectly delivered argument. Beliefs, experiences, relationships, questions, doubts, and assumptions often develop over many years.

Meaningful evangelism can unfold the same way.

Listen→ Understand→ Pray→ Learn→ Share→ Follow Up

Sometimes the next faithful step is an answer. Sometimes it is a question. Sometimes it is sharing a podcast two weeks later. Sometimes it is simply listening well enough that another conversation remains possible.

The aim is not to force a spiritual outcome. It is to faithfully cultivate opportunities for truth over time.

Why “GARDEN”?

The name reflects the kind of evangelism this project is trying to encourage.

A gardener cannot force a seed to grow. The gardener prepares soil, plants, waters, tends, protects, waits, and remains faithful to work that often develops slowly and beneath the surface.

Evangelism has similar limits. Christians can reason, explain, ask questions, share truth, pray, love people, and remain present. We cannot manufacture belief.

Our responsibility is faithfulness in planting and tending. The results ultimately belong to God.

What GARDEN Is Designed to Solve

“I don't know enough.”

You do not have to. Learn after the conversation and return later.

“I don't know what to say.”

Conversational models and thoughtful questions give you ways to engage without needing a speech.

“I forget what we've talked about.”

The Evangelism Profile Tool helps you remember conversations, prayer needs, resources shared, and possible next steps.

“I don't know who to trust.”

Trusted Voices and the Research Library point toward carefully selected Christian thinkers and resources.

“AI gives me answers, but can I trust them?”

The AI Frameworks give the tool theological and intellectual guardrails instead of treating generic AI output as authoritative.

“I'm uncomfortable talking about faith.”

GARDEN lets you begin at your actual stage of preparedness and grow gradually rather than pretending confidence you do not have.

A Toolkit, Not a Formula

GARDEN is not a script for converting people and it is not a sequence that every relationship must follow.

The tools exist to support faithful engagement: the GARDEN framework provides the overall approach; Your People encourages intentional prayer and follow-up; Conversational Models show what engagement can look like; Trusted Voices help users identify reliable teachers; the Research Library helps locate material on specific questions; and the AI Frameworks help believers use artificial intelligence more safely and productively for study and preparation.

Use what is helpful. Return to tools when you need them. The person in front of you is always more important than completing a process.

What We Believe About Truth

GARDEN is intentionally Christian. It is not designed to approach Christianity and competing worldviews from a position of theological neutrality.

The project operates within historic orthodox Christianity, with Scripture as the final authority for Christian belief and practice. At the same time, GARDEN encourages intellectual honesty: objections should be represented fairly, opposing arguments should be understood at their strongest, poor reasoning should not be attributed to someone merely because we disagree with them, and Christians should be willing to distinguish essential doctrines from legitimate secondary disagreements.

Confidence in Christianity should never require intellectual shortcuts.

What Success Looks Like

Success is not turning every Christian into an apologist.

It is a believer who used to remain silent but now listens carefully. A believer who is willing to say, “That's a good question. Let me think about it.” A believer who remembers what a friend said three months ago and follows up. A believer who prays specifically for someone. A believer who learns enough about one question to have a better conversation the next time it comes up.

And eventually, perhaps, a believer who realizes:

“I can actually do this.”

That is the kind of growth GARDEN is designed to cultivate.

Start With One Person

You do not need a ministry platform, a theology degree, or a list of strangers to approach.

Think of one person God has already placed in your life. Pray for them. Listen to them. Pay attention to what they believe and what they care about. And when an opportunity comes, take the next faithful step.
