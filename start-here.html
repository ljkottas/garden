Start exactly where you are

You don’t need to be ready. You just need to begin.

You do not need deep apologetics knowledge, quick answers, or confidence in difficult conversations to engage someone thoughtfully about faith. GARDEN gives you a low-pressure way to start with the people already in your life.

Permission to begin

You can say, “I don’t know.”

If someone asks a difficult question, you are allowed to think about it later.

“That’s a good question. I’m not sure how I would answer that right now. Let me think about it and get back to you.”

That is not a failed conversation. It can be the beginning of a better one.

Take the pressure off

Evangelism does not require an answer on the spot.

A lot of Christians stay silent because they imagine every spiritual conversation as a test: someone raises an objection, and they have a few seconds to produce the right answer. GARDEN starts from a different assumption. You can listen now and do the hard thinking later.

You don’t need

Expert knowledge

You can use trusted Christian thinkers, curated resources, and guided AI frameworks to help you learn after a question comes up.

You don’t need

Natural confidence

You can begin by listening. As your comfort grows, thoughtful questions can become your next conversational tool.

You don’t need

A perfect conversation

The goal is not to close the discussion in one sitting. Plant something worth thinking about and remain available for the next conversation.

The simplest way to start

Listen now. Prepare later. Follow up.

If you have no knowledge and no comfort, this is enough. You can practice the long game without pretending to be further along than you are.

Follow Up

The pressure is not on you to produce a conversion. Your role is to be faithful with the next opportunity.

Think like a gardener: cultivate, plant, water, pray—and trust God with the growth.

What this looks like

A real conversation can be this simple.

You hear an objection you cannot answer. Instead of panicking or avoiding the subject, you remember it and come back prepared.

✓
You listened.
You learned something important about what the other person believes.
✓
You prepared without pressure.
You had time to think, research, and find a useful resource.
✓
You created another conversation.
The first exchange did not need to do everything.
Friend: “If God is good, why does He allow children to suffer?”
You: “That’s a really good question. I’m not sure how I’d answer that right now. Let me think about it.”
Later, with no conversational pressure, you study the problem of suffering and find a helpful discussion from a trusted Christian thinker.
You, later: “I’ve been thinking about your question about suffering. I came across a discussion that helped me think about it differently. I’d be curious what you think.”
Grow at your own pace

Your conversations can develop as you do.

You do not have to jump from silence to expert-level apologetics. The Conversational Models show three common stages of preparedness and what faithful engagement can look like at each one.

Stage 1

No knowledge. No comfort.

Listen carefully. If asked a question, admit you do not know. Reflect, research, and follow up later.

“Let me think about that and get back to you.”
Stage 2

Growing comfort and curiosity.

Begin using questions to clarify claims, expose assumptions, test reasoning, and keep the burden on the person making the claim.

“What do you mean by that?”
Stage 3

Knowledge and confidence together.

Combine strategic questions with thoughtful explanations, corrections, and worldview analysis as the conversation requires.

Ask well. Answer when useful.

Your first mission field

Start with one person you already care about.

You do not need to approach strangers or manufacture spiritual encounters. Think about a friend, relative, coworker, neighbor, or someone else already in your life who does not believe.

Try this today

Choose one person.

Write down their name. Think about what you know about their story and beliefs. Remember any spiritual conversations you have already had. Then begin praying for them intentionally.

Keep it simple

Ask one question.

You might ask something as natural as, “What do you think happens when we die?” A good question can reveal a worldview, open a deeper discussion, and show you what you may want to prepare for later.
