The Evangelism Profile Tool

Your mission field is already in your life.

The people you hope will one day know Christ are often already in your contacts, at your workplace, across the street, around your dinner table, or beside you at your kids' activities.

See the profile ↓ View an example
The shift

From random moments to intentional relationships.

You are not creating a project around a person. You are simply remembering what matters so you can pray, listen, follow up, and engage more thoughtfully over time.

Why this tool exists

Good conversations are easy to lose.

A friend raises a question. A coworker mentions a painful experience. A family member makes a comment about faith. Then life moves on.

Weeks or months later, we may forget what was said, which resources we shared, or what we meant to follow up on. The Evangelism Profile Tool helps believers hold onto those moments without making relationships feel forced or mechanical.

The goal is simple: become more intentional with the people you already care about.

If we can keep detailed notes about work projects, finances, or hobbies, surely we can take a few minutes to think intentionally about the eternal future of the people we love.
People are not projects

Intentional does not mean manipulative.

This tool is not a sales pipeline, a scorecard, or a system for “working” people toward a result. It is a practical aid for remembering, praying, and following up with greater care.

Listen

Remember the whole person.

Background, experiences, pain, relationships, and beliefs all shape how someone hears spiritual ideas.

Pray

Pray specifically.

Move beyond generic prayers by remembering real struggles, questions, and circumstances.

Follow up

Continue naturally.

Remember what was discussed so a future question, resource, or conversation can build on what came before.

The profile

What to keep track of.

You do not need to fill out everything. Even a few useful notes can help. The template simply gives you categories to think through when they are relevant.

01

Basic Information

Name, relationship, how often you interact, and the context in which you know them.

02

Background

Important experiences, relationships, losses, church background, or life circumstances that may shape how they see the world.

03

Beliefs & Worldview

What they seem to believe about God, Christianity, truth, morality, science, meaning, or purpose.

04

Questions & Objections

Recurring issues or concerns such as suffering, biblical reliability, science and faith, hypocrisy, or religious pluralism.

05

Important Conversations

Short notes on meaningful discussions so future conversations can build on what has already happened.

06

Resources Shared

Videos, podcasts, articles, books, or ideas you have already passed along.

07

Prayer Notes

Specific ways you can pray for this person, their circumstances, and future opportunities.

08

Next Seed to Plant

The next natural step: ask a question, revisit a topic, share a resource, invite them somewhere, or simply continue building the relationship.

Example profile

What this might look like in real life.

The profile does not need to read like a report. Short notes are enough to help you remember the person, the conversation, and the next faithful step.

The details below are intentionally simple. The purpose is continuity, not documentation for its own sake.

Mark

Coworker
Background

Beliefs

Struggles / Questions

Prayer Notes
Pray for him through the divorce and for openness to spiritual questions.
Next Seed to Plant
Revisit the meaning/purpose conversation when it comes up naturally.
How it works

A small habit that supports the long game.

1. Notice

Pay attention to meaningful comments, questions, struggles, and spiritual openings.

2. Record

Later, jot down only what will help you remember and engage thoughtfully.

3. Pray

Use what you know to pray specifically for the person rather than abstractly.

4. Take the next step

Follow up, share a resource, ask another question, or simply continue the relationship.

Use the template

Keep it simple. Make it yours.

The eventual downloadable profile will be designed to work however you prefer—printed, copied into Word or Google Docs, or kept in your own notes system. Your personal information stays with you; the website does not need to store it.

Start small

Begin with one person.

You do not need profiles for everyone you know. Choose one person you already care about. Pray for them. Pay attention. Write down what matters. Then take the next faithful step when the opportunity comes.
