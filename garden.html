Why “GARDEN”?

Think like a gardener.

Gardeners prepare soil, plant, water, pay attention, and keep showing up. They participate faithfully in growth they cannot force.

Why this framework exists

Stop waiting until you feel ready.

Many Christians care deeply about people but assume evangelism requires extensive knowledge, quick thinking, or confidence in difficult conversations. So they wait—and often never begin.

GARDEN starts somewhere else. Listen carefully. Ask a question when you can. Think about the issue later. Find something helpful. Follow up. Pray. The framework grows with you as your knowledge and confidence grow.

Spiritual conversations often unfold over time. One person asks a question. Another shares a resource. Someone else models a faithful life. A later hardship changes what a person is willing to consider. GARDEN gives structure to that long game without turning relationships into projects or evangelism into a formula.

The six-part framework

One rhythm. Six practical habits.

You may not move through these in a perfect order. GARDEN is a mindset for relational evangelism, not a rigid sequence.

G
Grow Relationships

Begin with the people already in your life.

Most believers already have a mission field around them: coworkers, neighbors, friends, family members, parents from their kids' activities, people from hobbies, sports, and community life.

The goal is not to manufacture relationships for the sake of evangelism. It is to genuinely care about people and become more intentional within relationships that already exist. Trust creates opportunities that arguments rarely can.

Practice: Identify one person you already care about and begin praying for them intentionally.
A
Ask Thoughtful Questions

You do not need to start with answers.

Questions help you understand what someone actually means, uncover assumptions, clarify misunderstandings, and invite reflection. They also reduce pressure because the burden does not automatically fall on you to defend every claim in the moment.

“What do you mean by that?” and “How did you come to that conclusion?” can move a conversation further than a rushed counterargument.

Practice: When you hear a claim, try one clarifying question before offering a response.
R
Reflect & Research

Following up later is not a failure. It is part of the system.

You do not need to answer every question immediately. If a topic comes up and you do not know what to say, simply be honest: “That’s a good question. Let me think about it and get back to you.”

Then, when the pressure is gone, work through the issue. Consult trusted resources, use the External Engagement Framework, ask someone wiser, and prepare a thoughtful next step.

Practice: Write down one question or claim you heard recently and spend time understanding it this week.
D
Direct to Helpful Resources

You do not have to explain everything yourself.

Christians have access to gifted philosophers, theologians, biblical scholars, scientists, pastors, apologists, authors, and communicators who have spent years studying difficult questions.

A thoughtful video, podcast, article, book, or testimony can sometimes carry a conversation further than an improvised explanation.

Practice: Learn where to find one strong resource on a topic that comes up often in your relationships.
E
Engage Over Time

Think in months and years, not moments.

Meaningful conversations rarely happen in isolation. Trust develops over time. Questions develop over time. Worldviews often change over time.

A simple follow-up can reopen a door without pressure: “I was thinking about what you said the other day and came across something you might find interesting.”

Practice: Look for one natural opportunity to revisit a conversation rather than trying to create a brand-new one.
N
Nurture Through Prayer

Trust God with the growth and the harvest.

We can build relationships, ask questions, share ideas, provide resources, and continue conversations. We cannot change a heart.

Prayer keeps evangelism from becoming a technique. Pray for wisdom, opportunities, openness, courage, and the person—not merely for a successful conversation.

Practice: Pray specifically for one person's needs, questions, relationships, and openness to truth.

Designed for growth

The framework works at every stage of preparedness.

You can practice GARDEN when you know almost nothing, and continue practicing it after years of study. What changes is how deeply you can engage—not whether you can begin.

Stage 1

Listen. Prepare later.

No knowledge and no comfort? Pay attention, be honest about what you do not know, and return after you have had time to prepare.

You can begin here.
Stage 2

Ask. Clarify. Test.

As comfort grows, use questions to understand claims, expose assumptions, and keep the burden of proof where it belongs.

Questions become your tool.
Stage 3

Ask and answer.

As knowledge and confidence come together, combine strategic questions with thoughtful explanations, corrections, and worldview analysis.

Depth grows over time.
